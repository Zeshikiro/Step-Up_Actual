Chapter 1
THE PROBLEM AND ITS BACKGROUND
Physical activity is an essential component of health and well-being, particularly among young adults who are developing habits that may continue into later life. Regular movement contributes to the prevention and management of noncommunicable diseases, supports mental health, improves cognitive functioning, and enhances overall quality of life (World Health Organization [WHO], 2024). However, despite its recognized benefits, insufficient physical activity remains a continuing public health concern. The World Health Organization reported that a large proportion of adults worldwide do not meet the recommended levels of physical activity, while adolescents and young people continue to show high levels of inactivity (WHO, 2022, 2024). This concern is relevant to university students, whose academic workload, prolonged screen time, online activities, and sedentary routines may limit opportunities for regular physical movement.
In the Philippine context, physical inactivity also remains a concern. The National Nutrition Council reported that findings from the DOST-FNRI Expanded National Nutrition Survey showed a high prevalence of physical inactivity among Filipino adults, indicating the need for stronger health promotion strategies that encourage active lifestyles (National Nutrition Council, n.d.). Among university students, sedentary behavior may be influenced by class schedules, school requirements, technology use, and limited motivation to participate in physical activity. These conditions emphasize the need for accessible, engaging, and student-centered interventions that can encourage movement without requiring students to depend solely on structured exercise programs or gym-based activities.
The increasing use of mobile technologies provides an opportunity to promote health-related behaviors in a more accessible and interactive manner. Digital health interventions, including mobile applications, have been studied as possible tools for improving physical activity among college students. A systematic review and meta-analysis on digital health interventions for college students found that mobile and web-based interventions have been used to influence physical activity behavior, step counts, and sedentary behavior, although further research is still needed to determine how these interventions can be made more effective for this population (Wang et al., 2024). This suggests that mobile applications may serve as useful platforms for encouraging physical activity, especially when they are designed according to the needs, interests, and routines of students.
Gamification is one approach that can strengthen user engagement in fitness-related applications. It involves the use of game elements such as goals, feedback, badges, rewards, leaderboards, social interaction, and avatars in non-game settings to motivate users and support behavior change (Johnson et al., 2024). In physical activity applications, gamification may help make walking and step tracking more enjoyable by turning ordinary movement into a rewarding experience. However, existing studies also show that the effectiveness of digital and gamified health applications may vary depending on the design, target users, and level of engagement. This creates a research gap in developing localized applications that combine fitness tracking with game-like features specifically intended for university students.
Augmented reality also provides potential for increasing engagement in physical activity applications. AR-based mobile games have shown how digital content can be combined with real-world movement, location tracking, and exploration to encourage users to walk and interact with their surroundings. Studies on Pokémon GO, a location-based augmented reality game, suggest that AR mobile games can encourage physical activity by combining walking, exploration, and game mechanics (Khamzina et al., 2020; Lee et al., 2021). Nevertheless, related literature also indicates the need for more studies that examine how AR and location-based features can support sustained physical activity, especially in specific populations and local settings. This gap supports the development of an AR-based mobile application that is designed not only for entertainment but also for promoting physical activity among university students.
In response to these concerns, this study proposes STEP-UP: An Augmented Reality Mobile Application for Promoting Physical Activity Among University Students. The application is designed to encourage students to increase their physical activity through step tracking, GPS-based movement support, gamified missions, points, leaderboard ranking, avatar customization, health tips, and AR walking-related features. Instead of presenting physical activity as a purely routine task, STEP-UP aims to make walking more interactive, goal-oriented, and rewarding. By integrating mobile health concepts, gamification, and augmented reality, the application seeks to provide students with a more engaging way to monitor their steps and participate in movement-based activities.
The development of STEP-UP addresses the need for a localized and student-centered digital intervention within the university setting. While existing mobile fitness applications are widely available, many are general-purpose platforms that may not fully consider the academic lifestyle, motivation, and engagement patterns of university students. Furthermore, not all fitness applications combine step tracking, gamification, augmented reality, avatar-based rewards, and community-related features in a manner intended for campus-based users. Therefore, this study focuses on designing and developing a mobile application that promotes physical activity among students of Pampanga State Agricultural University while also evaluating the system based on its functionality, usability, and relevance to the target users.
Through this project, the researchers aim to contribute to the growing field of mobile health and gamified physical activity interventions by developing a system that is accessible, interactive, and appropriate for university students. The study is expected to provide insights into how augmented reality and gamification may be applied in a student-centered fitness application, particularly in encouraging walking and step-based activity. In this regard, STEP-UP is positioned as a capstone project that responds to current health concerns, technological opportunities, and research gaps related to physical inactivity, mobile fitness applications, and user engagement among university students.
Project Context
Pampanga State Agricultural University (PSAU) serves as the locale of this study, with its currently enrolled students as the target users of the STEP-UP application. University students are often exposed to academic demands, prolonged use of digital devices, and sedentary routines that may reduce their participation in regular physical activity. Although students may be aware of the importance of exercise, maintaining consistent motivation to move, walk, or engage in fitness-related activities can still be challenging. This situation provides an opportunity to develop a technology-based intervention that encourages students to become more active in a way that is accessible, engaging, and suitable to their daily academic environment.
The project entitled “STEP-UP: An Augmented Reality Mobile Application for Promoting Physical Activity Among University Students” is designed as a mobile application that encourages physical movement through step tracking, gamification, and augmented reality features. The application aims to transform walking and simple physical activity into an interactive experience by allowing users to monitor their progress, complete activity-based missions, earn points, customize avatars, and participate in leaderboard rankings. Through these features, STEP-UP seeks to provide students with a more motivating and enjoyable approach to physical activity.
The application is intended to support students in developing awareness of their daily movement patterns. By using the built-in step-tracking capability of Android smartphones, STEP-UP records the user’s step count and provides activity-related feedback. The system also includes features such as activity summaries, estimated calories burned, progress monitoring, and health-related information. These functions allow students to observe their physical activity performance and become more conscious of their movement habits.
To increase user engagement, STEP-UP integrates gamified elements such as points, rewards, missions, leaderboards, and avatar customization. These features are included to make the experience more interactive and goal-oriented rather than simply presenting step tracking as a routine activity. The leaderboard encourages friendly competition among users, while missions and rewards provide short-term goals that may motivate students to continue using the application. Avatar customization also gives users a sense of personalization, allowing them to connect their progress with visual in-app rewards.
Another important component of the project is the use of augmented reality and GPS-based features. Through AR-related interactions, the application aims to connect digital gameplay elements with real-world movement. This allows students to experience physical activity in a more immersive and interactive manner. The GPS-based features also support location-related movement tracking, making the application more suitable for walking-based activities within or around the university environment. These features help distinguish STEP-UP from ordinary fitness tracking applications by combining physical activity monitoring with interactive mobile technology.
The system also uses Firebase services for user authentication, database management, and data storage. This allows users to create accounts, save their progress, access activity records, and participate in system features such as leaderboard ranking and profile management. The use of cloud-based services supports real-time data handling and ensures that user activity information can be properly managed during the implementation and evaluation of the project.
In the context of this study, STEP-UP will serve as both a developed capstone project and a physical activity promotion tool for university students. The application is not intended to replace professional fitness programs, medical advice, or formal physical education activities. Instead, it is designed as a supportive and motivational platform that encourages students to increase movement through walking, step-based goals, and interactive features. The project focuses on promoting awareness, motivation, and engagement in physical activity through a mobile application that students can conveniently use with their smartphones.
Overall, STEP-UP responds to the need for a localized, student-centered, and technology-based approach to promoting physical activity. By combining mobile health concepts, gamification, augmented reality, and step tracking, the project aims to provide PSAU students with an accessible tool that encourages active behavior. The project context is therefore grounded in the current need to address sedentary habits among university students while utilizing mobile technology as a practical platform for health promotion.

This study aims to develop and evaluate STEP-UP: An Augmented Reality Mobile Application for Promoting Physical Activity Among University Students. Specifically, the study focuses on the development of a mobile application that integrates step tracking, augmented reality, gamification, and health guidance features to encourage physical activity among university students.
General Objective
The general objective of this study is to develop and evaluate an augmented reality mobile application, STEP-UP, designed to promote physical activity among university students.
Specific Objectives
Specifically, this study aims to:
Develop a mobile application that integrates automatic step tracking, augmented reality walking mode, gamification features, and health guidance tools. 
Compare the average daily step counts of the experimental group and control group during the intervention period. 
Determine whether the STEP-UP application significantly increases physical activity levels among university students. 
Assess changes in user motivation, autonomy, competence, and behavioral intention toward physical activity after using the application through constructs adapted from Self-Determination Theory (SDT). 
Evaluate user engagement, enjoyment, immersion, and gameplay experience of the application using selected constructs from the Game Experience Questionnaire (GEQ). 
Evaluate the software quality of the STEP-UP application using ISO/IEC 25010 standards in terms of functional suitability, usability, reliability, and performance efficiency.

Scope and Limitations of the Project
This section presents the coverage and boundaries of the study entitled “STEP-UP: An Augmented Reality Mobile Application for Promoting Physical Activity Among University Students.” It identifies the specific features, users, locale, and evaluation areas included in the project. It also discusses the limitations of the study to clarify the conditions, restrictions, and factors that may affect the development, implementation, and evaluation of the application.
Scope
This study focuses on the development and evaluation of STEP-UP, an Android-based augmented reality mobile application designed to promote physical activity among university students. The locale of the study is Pampanga State Agricultural University (PSAU), and the target population consists of currently enrolled students of the university. The respondents for the evaluation of the application will be selected from the PSAU student population, with the sample size to be determined using Slovin’s formula based on the official total student population.
The project covers the development of a mobile application that integrates automatic step tracking, augmented reality walking mode, gamification features, and health guidance tools. The application includes user registration and login, user profile management, daily step count monitoring, activity summaries, estimated calories burned, missions, points, rewards, avatar customization, leaderboard ranking, and health-related information. These features are intended to encourage students to become more aware of their physical activity and to motivate them to increase their daily movement through interactive and gamified experiences.
The study also covers the comparison of the average daily step counts of the experimental group and control group during the intervention period. The experimental group will use the STEP-UP application, while the control group will not use the application as part of the intervention. This comparison will be used to determine whether the application contributes to an increase in physical activity levels among university students.
Furthermore, the study includes the evaluation of user motivation, autonomy, competence, and behavioral intention toward physical activity using constructs adapted from Self-Determination Theory (SDT). It also includes the evaluation of user engagement, enjoyment, immersion, and gameplay experience using selected constructs from the Game Experience Questionnaire (GEQ). In addition, the software quality of the STEP-UP application will be assessed using selected characteristics of the ISO/IEC 25010 standard, specifically functional suitability, usability, reliability, and performance efficiency.
Limitations
This study is limited to the design, development, deployment, and evaluation of the STEP-UP mobile application within the context of Pampanga State Agricultural University. The study will only involve selected PSAU students as respondents; therefore, the findings may not fully represent students from other universities or educational institutions.
The application is designed primarily for Android smartphones. Students who do not have access to an Android device or whose phones do not support the required sensors, GPS, or augmented reality features may not be able to fully use the application. The accuracy of the step-tracking feature may also vary depending on the smartphone model, built-in sensors, device settings, and how the user carries the phone during movement.
The GPS-based and augmented reality features of the application may be affected by internet connectivity, location signal accuracy, device performance, lighting conditions, and environmental factors. The application also requires stable network access for features that depend on Firebase services, such as account authentication, database synchronization, leaderboard updates, and user activity records.
The study is also limited to walking and step-based physical activity. It does not measure all forms of exercise such as cycling, swimming, weight training, or sports activities. The application is not intended to serve as a medical device, clinical fitness assessment tool, or substitute for professional health advice. The health guidance provided by the application is limited to general information and is not designed to diagnose, treat, or manage medical conditions.
In addition, the evaluation of the application will be limited to the intervention period set by the researchers. The study will assess the short-term effect of the application on physical activity, motivation, engagement, and software quality. Long-term behavioral change, continuous app usage after the study period, and the long-term health outcomes of the users are beyond the scope of this study.
Definition of Terms
For better understanding of the study, the following terms are defined operationally:
Augmented Reality (AR). This refers to a technology that combines digital elements with the real-world environment. In this study, AR is used as part of the walking mode of the STEP-UP application to make physical activity more interactive and engaging.
Automatic Step Tracking. This refers to the feature of the application that records the number of steps taken by the user through the smartphone’s built-in sensors.
Behavioral Intention. This refers to the user’s willingness or intention to continue engaging in physical activity after using the STEP-UP application.
Control Group. This refers to the group of student respondents who will not use the STEP-UP application during the intervention period and will serve as the comparison group.
Experimental Group. This refers to the group of student respondents who will use the STEP-UP application during the intervention period.
Firebase. This refers to the cloud-based platform used in the application for user authentication, database management, and storage of user-related records such as profiles, activity data, and leaderboard information.
Game Experience Questionnaire (GEQ). This refers to an evaluation tool used to assess selected aspects of the user’s gameplay experience, including engagement, enjoyment, and immersion.
Gamification. This refers to the use of game-like elements such as missions, points, rewards, avatar customization, and leaderboards to encourage user motivation and participation.
GPS. This refers to the Global Positioning System used by the application to support location-based and movement-related features.
Health Guidance Tools. These refer to the health-related information and activity feedback provided by the application to help users become more aware of their physical activity habits.
ISO/IEC 25010. This refers to an international standard for evaluating software quality. In this study, selected characteristics such as functional suitability, usability, reliability, and performance efficiency will be used to assess the STEP-UP application.
Leaderboard. This refers to the ranking feature of the application that displays users based on their accumulated activity-related progress, such as steps or points.
Physical Activity. This refers to bodily movement that requires energy expenditure. In this study, physical activity mainly refers to walking and step-based movement recorded through the STEP-UP application.
Self-Determination Theory (SDT). This refers to a theory of motivation used in this study to assess changes in user motivation, autonomy, competence, and behavioral intention toward physical activity.
STEP-UP. This refers to the augmented reality mobile application developed in this study to promote physical activity among university students through step tracking, gamification, AR walking mode, and health guidance features.

2.1 Physical Inactivity and Sedentary Behavior Among University Students
Physical inactivity remains a significant health concern because regular physical activity contributes to physical health, mental well-being, disease prevention, and overall wellness. The World Health Organization (WHO) emphasized that physical activity provides important benefits to the body and mind, while insufficient physical activity increases the risk of noncommunicable diseases and other negative health outcomes (WHO, 2024). For adults, the WHO recommends at least 150 to 300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity per week, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous-intensity aerobic physical activity per week, or an equivalent combination of both (WHO, 2020). These recommendations indicate that physical activity does not have to be limited to structured exercise, since regular movement such as walking may also contribute to healthier lifestyle habits.
Despite these recommendations, physical inactivity remains a continuing global and local concern. The WHO reported that approximately 31% of adults worldwide, equivalent to about 1.8 billion people, did not meet the recommended levels of physical activity in 2022 (WHO, 2024). In the Philippine context, the National Nutrition Council reported that the DOST-FNRI Expanded National Nutrition Survey showed that physical inactivity among Filipino adults remained high, with a prevalence of 40.6% in 2018 (National Nutrition Council [NNC], n.d.). These findings show the need for practical and accessible health promotion strategies that encourage individuals, including students, to engage in regular movement-based activities.
University students may be vulnerable to physical inactivity and sedentary behavior because their academic responsibilities often involve long hours of sitting, studying, completing requirements, and using digital devices. Deliens et al. (2015) found that university students’ physical activity and sedentary behavior are influenced by academic pressure, examinations, university lifestyle, and living arrangements. Similarly, Moulin et al. (2021) emphasized that sedentary time among undergraduate students is an important issue because prolonged sitting and inactivity are common in higher education settings.
Previous studies also indicate the need for more structured interventions that can encourage students to increase their physical activity. Babaeer et al. (2022) reviewed studies on physical activity, sedentary behavior, and educational outcomes among university students and noted that existing evidence varies because many studies relied on self-reported measures and differed in research quality. This suggests the need for interventions that can support physical activity monitoring and encourage students to reduce sedentary habits through more engaging and measurable approaches.
In relation to the present study, these findings support the development of STEP-UP as a mobile-based intervention for promoting physical activity among university students. Since walking is one of the most accessible forms of physical activity, an application that records step counts and encourages daily movement may help students become more aware of their physical activity habits. The integration of automatic step tracking, gamification, and augmented reality walking mode addresses the need for a more engaging and student-centered approach to physical activity promotion. Therefore, the reviewed literature establishes the importance of addressing physical inactivity and sedentary behavior among university students through accessible, interactive, and technology-based solutions.
2.2 Physical Activity Promotion and Student Wellness
Physical activity promotion is important in supporting student wellness because regular movement contributes to physical health, mental well-being, and quality of life. The World Health Organization (WHO) explained that physical activity provides benefits for the heart, body, and mind, and may help reduce symptoms of depression and anxiety (WHO, 2024). These benefits are relevant to university students because academic requirements, prolonged sitting, and frequent screen use may affect their physical and mental well-being.
Physical activity does not have to be limited to sports, gym workouts, or structured exercise programs. The WHO (2020) emphasized that all forms of movement can contribute to health, including walking, active recreation, and other moderate physical activities. This is important in the university setting because walking is one of the most accessible forms of physical activity that students can perform during their daily routines, such as moving between classrooms, walking around campus, or completing simple step-based goals.
Studies also show that physical activity interventions can support student wellness. Donnelly et al. (2024) found that physical activity interventions in higher education may improve at least one measure of mental health or quality of life. Similarly, Li and Huang (2025) reported that physical activity is generally associated with better mental well-being among college students. These studies suggest that encouraging students to become more physically active may help support both their physical health and psychological wellness.
In relation to the present study, these findings support the development of STEP-UP as a mobile application that promotes walking and physical activity among university students. By using step tracking, progress monitoring, missions, rewards, and health guidance, the application aims to make physical activity more accessible and engaging. The reviewed literature supports the idea that promoting simple and regular movement, such as walking, can contribute to student wellness and serve as a practical approach to reducing sedentary behavior in the university setting.
2.3 Mobile Health Applications and Digital Physical Activity Interventions
Mobile health applications have become useful tools for supporting health-related behaviors because they are accessible, portable, and commonly used through smartphones. The World Health Organization (WHO, 2021) recognized digital health as an important approach for improving health services and supporting health promotion through digital technologies. In the context of physical activity, mobile applications can provide activity monitoring, goal setting, reminders, feedback, and progress tracking, which may help users become more aware of their movement habits.
Smartphones are suitable for physical activity interventions because they contain built-in features such as sensors, internet connectivity, and location services. These features allow applications to monitor steps, record user progress, and provide feedback based on user activity. Romeo et al. (2019) found that smartphone applications may help increase physical activity, although the effectiveness of these applications depends on their design and the level of user engagement.
Digital health interventions have also been studied among college students. Bi et al. (2024) reported that digital health interventions can significantly increase step counts among college students, showing that mobile-based approaches may be useful in encouraging physical activity in higher education settings. However, the study also suggests that not all physical activity outcomes improve equally, which means that application design and user motivation remain important considerations.
In relation to the present study, these findings support the development of STEP-UP as a mobile health application for promoting physical activity among university students. Instead of relying only on activity monitoring, STEP-UP integrates automatic step tracking, gamification, augmented reality walking mode, rewards, leaderboard ranking, and health guidance. These features aim to make physical activity monitoring more engaging and suitable for student users.
2.4 Smartphone-Based Step Tracking and Activity Monitoring
Smartphone-based step tracking is a practical method for monitoring physical activity because many students already use smartphones in their daily routines. Modern smartphones contain built-in sensors, such as accelerometers and step counters, that can detect movement and estimate the number of steps taken by the user. Straczkiewicz et al. (2021) explained that smartphones can be used in health research to measure physical activity and sedentary behavior, although the accuracy of measurements may vary depending on phone placement, device orientation, walking speed, and user behavior.
Step count is commonly used in physical activity monitoring because it is simple, understandable, and easy for users to track. Paluch et al. (2022) found that higher daily step counts were associated with lower all-cause mortality among adults, which supports the use of step count as a useful indicator of physical activity. In mobile applications, step count can also help users monitor progress, set goals, and become more aware of their daily movement.
However, smartphone-based step tracking also has limitations. Case et al. (2015) found that smartphone applications and wearable devices may differ in step count accuracy, although some smartphone applications produced results close to observed steps. Parmenter et al. (2022) also emphasized that smartphone-based physical activity measurement can be affected by device type, sensor quality, and how the phone is carried by the user. These findings show that step tracking is useful, but it should be treated as an activity indicator rather than a clinical measurement.
In relation to the present study, STEP-UP uses smartphone-based step tracking to monitor students’ walking activity and support progress-based features. The recorded step count is used for daily goals, weekly missions, estimated calories burned, rewards, activity summaries, and leaderboard ranking. Since the study focuses on promoting physical activity among university students, step tracking provides a simple and measurable way to encourage students to become more aware of their movement habits.
2.5 Gamification and Motivation in Fitness Applications
Gamification refers to the use of game-like elements in non-game settings to improve user motivation, engagement, and participation. In fitness and mobile health applications, gamification is commonly applied through points, rewards, badges, missions, progress bars, leaderboards, challenges, and avatars. These features are used to make physical activity more interactive and goal-oriented, especially for users who may lack motivation to exercise regularly.
Gamification has been studied as a strategy for encouraging physical activity through mobile health applications. Xu et al. (2022) explained that gamified mobile health interventions may support physical activity by using features such as goal setting, feedback, rewards, and progress monitoring. These elements can help users become more aware of their activity and may encourage them to continue participating in health-related behaviors.
Several studies also support the potential of gamification in improving physical activity outcomes. Yang et al. (2022) found that gamified smartphone applications can have a small-to-moderate effect on physical activity, suggesting that game-based features may help encourage movement when properly integrated into mobile applications. Similarly, Mazeas et al. (2022) reported that gamified interventions may improve physical activity, although maintaining long-term engagement remains a challenge.
In relation to the present study, these findings support the integration of gamification features in STEP-UP. The application includes daily goals, weekly missions, points, rewards, avatar customization, progress monitoring, and leaderboard ranking to make walking more engaging for university students. These features are intended to encourage users to complete physical activity tasks, monitor their progress, and become more motivated to participate in step-based activities.
The reviewed literature shows that gamification can support physical activity promotion, but it should be designed carefully to avoid becoming only a temporary source of interest. For this reason, STEP-UP combines gamification with automatic step tracking, augmented reality walking mode, and health guidance. This combination allows the application to support both motivation and activity monitoring while providing a more interactive experience for student users.
2.6 Augmented Reality and Location-Based Technologies in Physical Activity
Augmented reality is a technology that combines virtual elements with the real-world environment, allowing users to interact with digital objects through a device such as a smartphone. Azuma (1997) described augmented reality as a system that combines real and virtual content, allows real-time interaction, and aligns virtual objects with the physical environment. In mobile applications, augmented reality can be supported by cameras, sensors, GPS, and location-based services to create interactive experiences connected to real-world movement.
Location-based technologies are also useful in promoting physical activity because they allow applications to respond to the user’s real-world location. Through GPS and map-based services, mobile applications can encourage users to walk, explore, and complete movement-related tasks in actual environments. This makes location-based technology relevant to physical activity applications because it connects digital interaction with real-world walking and movement.
Several studies have shown the potential of augmented reality and location-based applications in encouraging physical activity. Pokémon GO is one of the most common examples because it uses GPS, augmented reality, and gamified tasks to encourage users to walk and explore. Althoff et al. (2016) found that Pokémon GO increased physical activity among users, particularly during the early period of use. Similarly, Khamzina et al. (2020) reported that Pokémon GO was associated with short-term increases in physical activity, although long-term effects were less consistent.
Lee et al. (2021) also discussed how Pokémon GO encouraged physical activity through real-world exploration and game-based interaction. However, the study also emphasized that the effect of such applications may depend on continued engagement. This suggests that augmented reality and location-based features can help encourage movement, but they should be supported by other motivational features to maintain user interest.
In relation to the present study, these findings support the use of augmented reality walking mode and location-based features in STEP-UP. The application uses AR and GPS-supported interaction to make walking more engaging for university students. By combining augmented reality with step tracking, missions, rewards, and leaderboard ranking, STEP-UP aims to encourage students to participate in physical activity through a more interactive and student-centered mobile experience.
2.7 Theoretical and Evaluation Frameworks Related to the Study
The evaluation of STEP-UP is supported by Self-Determination Theory, the Game Experience Questionnaire, and ISO/IEC 25010. These frameworks were selected because the study evaluates the application from three important perspectives: student motivation toward physical activity, user experience with the gamified and augmented reality features, and software quality of the mobile application.
Self-Determination Theory (SDT) explains motivation based on the psychological needs of autonomy, competence, and relatedness. According to Deci and Ryan (2000), individuals are more likely to engage in an activity when they feel that they have control over their actions, believe that they are capable of improving, and experience meaningful support or connection. In the present study, SDT is used to assess students’ motivation, autonomy, competence, and behavioral intention toward physical activity before and after the intervention.
SDT is relevant to STEP-UP because the application is designed to encourage students to become more physically active through self-paced walking, step goals, progress monitoring, missions, rewards, and health guidance. The autonomy aspect is reflected in allowing students to walk at their own pace and manage their own activity habits. The competence aspect is supported through step tracking, activity summaries, and goal completion, while motivation and behavioral intention are assessed to determine whether students become more willing to continue participating in physical activity.
The Game Experience Questionnaire (GEQ) is used to evaluate the user experience of game-based or gamified systems. IJsselsteijn et al. (2013) developed the GEQ to measure different aspects of game experience, such as immersion, competence, affect, and engagement-related experiences. In this study, selected GEQ-based constructs are used to assess students’ engagement, enjoyment, immersion, and gameplay experience after using STEP-UP.
The GEQ is relevant because STEP-UP includes gamified and interactive features such as missions, points, rewards, leaderboard ranking, avatar customization, and augmented reality walking mode. These features are intended to make walking more engaging and enjoyable for students. By using GEQ-based items, the researchers can evaluate whether the game-like and AR features of the application contributed to a positive user experience.
ISO/IEC 25010 provides a software quality model used to evaluate the quality characteristics of software products. The model includes characteristics such as functional suitability, usability, reliability, and performance efficiency (International Organization for Standardization [ISO], 2011). In the present study, these selected characteristics are used to evaluate whether STEP-UP provides the required features, is easy to use, performs consistently, and responds efficiently on supported Android devices.
In relation to the present study, these three frameworks provide a complete basis for evaluating STEP-UP. SDT focuses on the motivational effect of the application, GEQ focuses on the user’s experience with the gamified and AR features, and ISO/IEC 25010 focuses on the technical quality of the software. Through these frameworks, the study can assess not only whether STEP-UP functions as a mobile application, but also whether it supports student motivation and provides an engaging physical activity experience.

BMI, Estimated Calories Burned, and Step Recommendation - The system allows users to input their height and weight to compute their Body Mass Index (BMI). Vehicle Detection Anti-Cheat Mechanism - The application includes a speed-threshold mechanism intended to reduce inaccurate step recording during vehicle movement. When movement speed exceeds the set threshold, the system may pause or limit step counting to help maintain the accuracy of recorded activity.
Safety Reminders - STEP-UP provides safety reminders to encourage users to remain aware of their surroundings while using the application. These reminders help promote responsible use of the application, especially during walking activities and augmented reality interactions.
These features support the main purpose of STEP-UP, which is to encourage physical activity among university students through a combination of step tracking, gamification, augmented reality, progress monitoring, and health-related guidance. The features also provide the basis for evaluating the application in terms of functionality, user engagement, motivation, and software quality.
3.8 Project Management
The development of STEP-UP followed an Agile development methodology. This approach was used because the project required continuous planning, development, testing, and improvement of the mobile application. Since STEP-UP includes several modules such as user registration, BMI profiling, step tracking, gamification, avatar customization, Firebase integration, Mapbox and GPS services, augmented reality walking mode, leaderboard, and companion website, the researchers divided the development process into smaller development phases.
Through Agile methodology, the researchers were able to work on specific features one at a time, test the implemented functions, identify errors, and apply necessary improvements. This process helped the team revise the application based on technical issues, testing results, and adviser recommendations. The use of Agile also allowed the researchers to improve the system gradually instead of developing all components in a single phase.
Development logs were maintained to record completed tasks, encountered problems, system changes, and planned improvements. GitHub was also used for version control to organize the source code, monitor revisions, and reduce the risk of file loss or project corruption. This helped the researchers manage the development process and maintain an organized record of the application’s progress.
3.8.1 Agile Development Methodology
The Agile development methodology was applied by dividing the development of STEP-UP into short and focused development sprints. Each sprint focused on a specific part of the application, such as the user interface, security and backend integration, hardware sensor implementation, gamification, avatar customization, cloud saving, map integration, augmented reality features, and final system polishing.
Each sprint followed a repeated cycle of planning, designing, developing, testing, and revising. After completing a feature or module, the researchers tested its functionality and identified issues that needed improvement. Necessary revisions were then applied before proceeding to the next development activity. This allowed the team to continuously improve the application throughout the development period.
The Agile approach was appropriate for STEP-UP because the application required several technical components to work together. Features such as step tracking, GPS, AR walking mode, rewards, leaderboard, and cloud synchronization needed to be tested and adjusted repeatedly to ensure that they functioned properly on supported Android devices. Through this method, the researchers were able to prepare the system for deployment and user testing.
3.8.2 Development Phases
The development of STEP-UP was divided into several phases based on the major components of the application. The first phase focused on the user interface foundation and routing. During this phase, the researchers developed the main screens and user flow of the application, including the login, End-User License Agreement, BMI, and profile-related screens. Interface scaling was also considered to ensure that the application could display properly on supported Android devices.
The second phase focused on security and backend integration. Firebase was integrated to support user authentication, account sessions, and data management. Local data storage was also implemented to support selected settings and user-related records. This phase helped establish the foundation for account management, profile records, and data synchronization.
The third phase involved hardware sensor integration and gamification. The researchers tested and integrated Android device sensors, including the step counter and accelerometer, to support automatic step tracking. Step-related functions were developed to monitor user movement, support daily step goals, and connect user progress with gamified features such as missions and rewards.
The fourth phase focused on avatar customization. The avatar system was improved to support user personalization and reward-based customization. This allowed users to customize their avatars using earned points or coins, making the application more interactive and engaging.
The fifth phase involved Mapbox, ARCore, and cloud saving features. Mapbox and GPS services were integrated to support map-based and location-related functions, while ARCore was used to support the augmented reality walking mode. Firebase cloud services were also used to synchronize selected user records, including step data, avatar data, rewards, and progress records.
The sixth phase focused on the companion web application and leaderboard-related features. The supporting website was developed to present project information and display selected application-related data. This phase also supported the visibility of user progress and ranking through Firebase-connected records.
The final phase involved compliance, testing, polishing, and system improvement. The researchers addressed technical issues, improved application performance, revised input handling, and prepared the application for deployment and user testing. Final adjustments were made to ensure that STEP-UP was stable, usable, and aligned with the project objectives.
